World Wars & Uganda

Exploring Uganda's historical experience during World War I and World War II, including military participation, economic effects and social changes.

Uganda and the World Wars

World War I

World War I took place from 1914 to 1918. Uganda was then a British protectorate. People and resources from East Africa became involved in the wider war.

World War II

World War II lasted from 1939 to 1945. Uganda again contributed people, agricultural products and other resources to the British war effort.

East African Forces

The King's African Rifles was a British colonial regiment drawing soldiers from several East African territories, including Uganda.

World War I — 1914–1918

1914 — War begins

The First World War began in Europe in 1914 but quickly became a global conflict. Fighting also took place in East Africa.

East African Campaign

East Africa became an important theatre of the war. The King's African Rifles participated in military operations in German East Africa.

Ugandan involvement

Uganda contributed personnel and resources within the British colonial system. The wider East African war also affected civilian communities through labour demands, transportation and shortages.

1918 — War ends

The First World War ended in November 1918. Its economic and social consequences continued to affect East Africa after the fighting ended.

World War II — 1939–1945

King's African Rifles

The King's African Rifles included units associated with Uganda. The Imperial War Museums records the 4th (Uganda) Battalion and its association with World War II.

Military Service

East African troops served in different theatres during World War II. The regiment fought in areas including Madagascar and Burma.

Economic Contribution

Uganda's economy was connected to the British wartime system. Agricultural production, transport and labour were important parts of the colonial war effort.

Effects on Uganda

1. Military Experience

Ugandan and other East African soldiers gained military experience through service in colonial forces.

2. Economic Effects

Wartime demand affected agriculture, trade, transport and labour in Uganda.

3. Social Effects

Recruitment and wartime labour requirements affected families and communities.

4. Political Awareness

The wider experience of colonial service and global conflict formed part of the historical background in which later political developments occurred.
